# What makes Monday Night Miracles different

The recap and this post share a byline and a pipeline. They do not share a job.

|  | Weekly Recap | Monday Night Miracles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Runs | Tuesday, after the week settles | Monday morning, mid-week |
| Tense | Past. It happened. | **Present and future. It has not.** |
| Length | 1,500–2,200 | 900–1,400 |
| Source | Settled awards and final scores | Live scores and who is still on the field |
| Job | Tell them what happened | Tell them what to watch for tonight |
| Register | Aftermath, autopsy, verdict | Desperation, sweat, prayer |

## The trap

Everything in the recap's world is settled, so its prose can be confident.
Nothing here is. **The single most likely failure is writing a Monday post in
the recap's voice** — calling a result, describing a manager as having blown
something, eulogising a season — and then having the underdog win by nine.

The test: read every sentence and ask *"does this still make sense if the
opposite happens tonight?"* If not, it is a prediction and has to be dressed as
one. Burner may be as loud and as certain as he likes about what he THINKS will
happen. He may not report it as though it has.

## What the series is actually for

Three managers are going to spend Monday evening doing arithmetic in their
heads. This post is for them, and for the nine watching. It should name:

- the exact number somebody needs
- the exact player who has to produce it
- what it costs them in the table if it does not happen

A deficit is abstract. "Jalen Hurts has to be held under 3.3 points" is a thing
you can watch for, and it turns one unremarkable Monday game into appointment
television for a fantasy league that is not otherwise involved in it.

## Handing off to Tuesday

The recap follows this post by about thirty hours and will know every answer
this one had to guess at. Make that worth something: state what you expect
clearly enough that Tuesday can quote it back. A Monday prediction that lands
is the best callback material the recap gets all week, and one that misses is
better.
