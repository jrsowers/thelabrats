# The Lab Rats — League Rules & Draft Primer
### 2026 Season · 12 Teams · Everything you need before you draft

Read this once and you will know more than you need to. The short version is at
the top; the detail is underneath.

Every section ends with **What that means for you** — how the rule might change
how you draft or manage. That part is advice, not law. Ignore it freely.

> ⚠️ **Anything marked like this differs from ESPN's default settings.** Those
> are the ones worth reading twice, because your instincts from other leagues
> will be wrong.

## 1. League Format

- **12 teams**, one division, everybody plays everybody.
- **Head-to-head, points-based.** Highest score that week wins the matchup.
- ⚠️ **Ties are real.** There is no tiebreaker inside a regular-season matchup.
  If you and your opponent land on exactly the same score, you each take a tie,
  and records are kept as **W–L–T**.
- **Public league.** No password, and the league page is viewable by anyone.

### What that means for you

A tie is worth half a win, so a lineup decision that changes your score by 0.4
points genuinely can matter. In practice this comes up once or twice a season
and always to somebody's fury.

## 2. Roster Requirements

### Starting lineup — 10 spots

| Slot | Who is eligible |
| --- | --- |
| QB | Quarterbacks |
| RB × 2 | Running backs |
| WR × 2 | Wide receivers |
| TE | Tight ends |
| ⚠️ **OP** | **Any offensive player — including a second QB** |
| FLEX | RB / WR / TE |
| D/ST | Team defense |
| K | Kicker |

### Bench and IR

- **5 bench spots.** That is all.
- **1 IR spot**, for a player ESPN has flagged as out or on injured reserve.
- **15 total roster spots**, which is exactly the length of the draft.

### Roster caps by position

⚠️ You cannot hoard a position. The league enforces maximums:

| QB | RB | WR | TE | K | D/ST |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 8 | 8 | 3 | 2 | 2 |

### What that means for you

**The OP slot makes this a Superflex league.** That is the single most
important thing on this page.

In a normal league you start one quarterback, so QBs are replaceable and most
people wait. Here, effectively **every team starts two**. Twelve teams × two
starting QBs means **24 quarterbacks are starting every week**, and there are
not 24 good ones. The drop-off from QB12 to QB24 is a cliff, not a slope.

Practical consequences:

- Quarterbacks go **much earlier** than you are used to. Elite QBs belong in
  the first two rounds here, not the sixth.
- **Waiting on QB is a real strategy, but it is a plan, not an accident.** If
  you intend to punt the position you need to know that by pick 30, not
  discover it in round 9.
- Ending the draft with **one** quarterback means starting a replacement-level
  player in your OP slot every week, or streaming from a waiver pool that
  eleven other managers are also picking through.
- The cap of **4 QBs** stops anyone cornering the market, but three is a
  genuinely defensible number here.

**Five bench spots is tight.** With 10 starters and only 5 reserves, you have
almost no room to stash injured players, handcuff your own running backs, or
speculate on a rookie. Every bench spot you spend on a lottery ticket is a spot
you do not have when three of your starters are questionable on a Sunday
morning. Late-round picks that cannot help you in September are expensive.

**Only 3 TE spots and 2 K/DST spots**, so streaming those positions means
dropping as you go rather than rostering options.

## 3. Scoring Settings

### Passing

| Event | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Passing yards | 1 per 25 (0.04/yd) |
| ⚠️ **Passing touchdown** | **6** |
| ⚠️ **Interception thrown** | **−1** |
| 2-point conversion pass | 2 |
| ⚠️ 400+ yard passing game | **+2 bonus** |

### Rushing

| Event | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Rushing yards | 1 per 10 (0.1/yd) |
| Rushing touchdown | 6 |
| 2-point conversion run | 2 |
| ⚠️ 200+ yard rushing game | **+2 bonus** |

### Receiving

| Event | Points |
| --- | --- |
| ⚠️ **Reception** | **0.5 (half PPR)** |
| Receiving yards | 1 per 10 (0.1/yd) |
| Receiving touchdown | 6 |
| 2-point conversion catch | 2 |
| ⚠️ 200+ yard receiving game | **+2 bonus** |

### Everything else on offense

| Event | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Fumble lost | −2 |
| Fumble recovered for a touchdown | 6 |
| Any return touchdown (kick, punt, INT, fumble) | 6 |
| 2-point conversion return | 2 |
| 1-point safety | 1 |

### Kicking

| Event | Points |
| --- | --- |
| FG under 40 yards | 3 |
| ⚠️ FG 40–49 yards | **3** |
| ⚠️ FG 50–59 yards | **5** |
| ⚠️ FG 60+ yards | **6** |
| Extra point | 1 |
| Missed FG under 40 | −1 |
| Missed FG 40–49 | −0.5 |
| Missed FG 50+ | 0 |
| Missed extra point | −1 |

### Team defense / special teams

**Points allowed**

| 0 | 1–6 | 7–13 | 14–17 | 18–27 | 28–34 | 35–45 | 46+ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| +10 | +8 | +6 | +3 | 0 | −1 | −3 | −5 |

⚠️ **Yards allowed** — a second, separate scoring axis:

| <100 | 100–199 | 200–299 | 300–399 | 400–449 | 450–549 | 550+ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| +5 | +3 | +1 | 0 | −1 | −3 | −5 |

**Plays**

| Sack | Interception | Fumble recovery | Blocked kick | Safety | Any TD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 6 |

### What that means for you

**Six-point passing TDs is the second half of the Superflex story.** ESPN's
default is 4. Moving to 6 adds roughly 50 points across a season to a
30-touchdown quarterback — and it compounds with the OP slot. Any ranking list
you pull off the internet is built for 4-point passing TDs and is therefore
**understating quarterbacks twice over**. Adjust upward, or use a Superflex list
and adjust upward from there.

**Interceptions only cost −1**, half the usual penalty. Volume gunslingers who
throw three picks a month are less risky here than their reputation suggests.

**Half PPR sits between the two formats you know.** Pure volume receivers lose
some of the value they hold in full PPR; big-play receivers and running backs
who catch passes hold most of theirs. Do not draft off a full-PPR board.

**The bonuses are small but real, and they reward ceiling over floor.** A 400-yard
passing game, a 200-yard rushing game and a 200-yard receiving game each pay
+2. Nobody should draft *for* these, but they very quietly favour the boom
player over the steady one, and they occasionally decide a matchup.

**Kickers are worth slightly more attention than usual** — barely. The 50+ and
60+ brackets pay 5 and 6, so a strong-legged kicker in a dome or on a team that
stalls near midfield has genuine upside. Note the flat 3 points for anything
under 50, which makes accuracy on short kicks worth less than distance.

**Defenses score on two axes here, not one.** Most leagues only count points
allowed. We also score yards allowed, and the swing is ±5. A defense that gets
shredded for 500 yards but bends-not-breaks to 20 points is worth much less
here than elsewhere. Favour defenses that are genuinely good, not just ones
with a favourable schedule.

## 4. Transaction Settings

### Waivers

- ⚠️ **Traditional rolling waiver priority. This is not a FAAB/budget league.**
- Everyone starts with a waiver priority based on draft order; **claim a player
  and you drop to the bottom of the list.**
- Dropped players sit on waivers for **2 days (48 hours)** before clearing.
- Claims process **overnight, six days a week** — Monday, Wednesday, Thursday,
  Friday, Saturday and Sunday. **There is no Tuesday run.**
- ESPN starts processing around **3:00 a.m. ET** and works through every league
  on the platform, so results sometimes do not land until **6:00 a.m. ET or
  later**. Check your roster in the morning, not at midnight.
- ⚠️ **Wednesday morning is the big one.** A player dropped over the weekend
  clears his 48 hours on Tuesday, and Tuesday is not a processing day — so the
  whole weekend's worth of waiver claims settles Wednesday.
- ⚠️ **The moment a batch finishes, everyone left unclaimed becomes a free
  agent** — first come, first served, no priority involved.
- **No limit** on the number of adds, drops or claims, in a week or a season.
- ESPN's **undroppable player list is on**, so you cannot drop a genuine star to
  clear a bench spot.

### Trades

- ⚠️ **No league voting.** Members cannot vote a trade down.
- Trades sit for a **1-day review period**, then process automatically.
- ⚠️ The **commissioner is the only person who can veto**, and processes trades
  promptly so nobody's roster sits frozen while twelve busy people forget to
  vote.
- If a trade looks genuinely out of whack, it gets **raised publicly with the
  league** and discussed openly — not quietly killed.
- **No limit** on the number of trades.
- ⚠️ **Trade deadline: Wednesday, 2 December 2026, 12:00 p.m. ET (noon).**
- Draft picks cannot be traded.

### Lineups

- Players lock **individually at their own kickoff**, not all at once on Sunday
  morning. A Monday night player can be swapped in on Monday afternoon.
- Unlimited lineup changes.

### What that means for you

**Waiver priority is a resource you spend exactly once.** With no FAAB, you
cannot outbid anyone — you can only decide whether this player is worth going
to the back of the queue for. Early in the season, the temptation is to burn
the #1 priority on a week-1 hero. Weigh that against the genuinely
league-winning player who emerges in week 6, when you would rather still be
near the top.

Because priority only moves when you **successfully claim**, sitting patiently
is a real strategy. Free agents are unclaimed after 48 hours and cost nothing
but a roster spot.

**Six runs a week means you are rarely stuck.** If you miss Wednesday,
Thursday is right there. The pool moves fast, so bye-week patching is very
doable — another reason not to hoard depth on five bench spots.

**Two different games are being played, and it is worth knowing which one you
are in.** Before a batch processes, waiver *priority* decides who gets the
player, and being awake makes no difference. The instant that batch finishes,
everyone unclaimed flips to free agency and it becomes **first come, first
served** — priority is irrelevant and speed is everything.

So: if you genuinely want a player, claim him and spend the priority. If you
are only mildly interested, wait for the batch to clear and grab him for free —
accepting that somebody quicker may beat you to it.

**Trades are meant to happen here.** No voting means no politics and no
hostage-taking, and the 24-hour window exists so people can see what happened,
not so they can block it. If you think a trade is bad for the league, say so out
loud rather than quietly.

**Individual game locks reward paying attention.** If your running back is a
surprise inactive at 11:58 on Sunday, you can still move a 4:25 player into the
slot. Check inactives.

## 5. Schedule & Playoffs

### Regular season

- **Weeks 1–13.** One opponent per week.

### Playoffs

- **6 teams qualify** — the top 6 by record.
- **Seeds 1 and 2 receive a first-round bye.**
- ⚠️ **Playoff seeding tiebreaker is head-to-head record.**
- ⚠️ **No reseeding.** The bracket is fixed once it is set, so a lower seed that
  wins does not get bumped to face the top remaining seed.

| Round | Weeks | Who plays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quarterfinals | 14 | 3v6, 4v5 |
| Semifinals | 15 | 1 and 2 enter |
| ⚠️ **Championship** | **16 and 17** | **Two weeks, combined score** |

- Teams that miss the playoffs play a **consolation bracket** rather than
  nothing.
- ⚠️ Once you are eliminated from contention for 5th place, **your transactions
  are locked** — you cannot add or drop to help or hinder anyone else's title
  run.

### What that means for you

**The championship is a two-week matchup, scored as a total.** This is the
deviation most likely to catch someone out. It means:

- One bad week does not end you, and one huge week does not save you. **Depth
  and consistency matter more than a single ceiling game.**
- Your championship roster needs to be healthy across **two** weeks, not one.
- Late-season NFL rest and load management becomes a real factor. Check which
  contenders have already clinched by week 17.

**A first-round bye is worth a lot.** Two of six playoff teams skip an entire
elimination game. That makes the difference between the 2 seed and the 3 seed
much larger than the difference between the 3 and the 4 — worth remembering in
week 13 when you are deciding whether to rest anyone or push.

**No reseeding means the bracket is knowable in advance.** Once seeds are set,
you know your exact path. If you are the 4 seed, you know precisely who you
would meet, and you can look at their week 15 and 16–17 schedules.

**Head-to-head is the seeding tiebreaker**, so the game against the team you are
level with genuinely counts twice.

## 6. Draft Day

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **When** | Thursday, 3 September 2026, 1:00 p.m. ET |
| **Type** | Snake (order reverses each round) |
| **Rounds** | 15 |
| **Clock** | 60 seconds per pick |
| **Draft order** | Set manually by the league manager |
| **Draft pick trading** | Not enabled |

### What that means for you

**Sixty seconds is not long.** Have a queue set before the draft starts, and
keep it deeper than you think you need — three or four picks ahead. If you are
away from your screen the autodrafter takes over, and it drafts strictly by
ESPN's board, which does not know we play Superflex with 6-point passing TDs.
It will take you a running back you did not want.

**Fifteen rounds fills your roster exactly.** There are no throwaway picks; the
last pick of your draft is on your team on Sunday.

## 7. Looking ahead: keepers

⚠️ **The league is set to allow 2 keepers next season.** No keepers this year —
2026 is a clean slate — but the players you draft now can carry into 2027.
Keepers lock one hour before the 2027 draft.

### What that means for you

If that setting stands, a late-round rookie who breaks out is worth more than
his 2026 production, because you may keep him. It is a reason to prefer the
young ascending player over the veteran with one good year left, **when the
cost is otherwise similar**. Do not let it distort your first five rounds; win
this year first.

## The deviations, in one list

Everything below differs from a default ESPN league. If you skim one section,
skim this one.

1. **Superflex (OP slot).** Effectively two starting QBs. Biggest change here.
2. **6-point passing touchdowns**, not 4. Compounds the above.
3. **Interceptions cost −1**, not −2.
4. **0.5 PPR**, not 0 or 1.
5. **Bonus points** for 400-yard passing, 200-yard rushing and 200-yard
   receiving games.
6. **Long field goals pay more** — 5 for 50–59, 6 for 60+.
7. **Defenses score on yards allowed as well as points allowed.**
8. **Traditional rolling waivers, not FAAB.**
9. **No trade voting or vetoes**; commissioner processes after a 24-hour review.
10. **Two-week championship** (weeks 16–17), combined score.
11. **No playoff reseeding**; head-to-head is the seeding tiebreaker.
12. **Roster caps** of 4 QB / 8 RB / 8 WR / 3 TE / 2 K / 2 D/ST.
13. **2 keepers next season.**
14. **Transactions lock for teams eliminated from 5th place** during playoffs.
